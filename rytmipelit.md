# Rhythm Games
Rhythm games are a genre of video game which involve the player making inputs in sync to a song, sometimes simulating playing an instrument. From the first commercial rhythm game Dance Aerobics to the first arcade rhythm game DanceDanceRevolution, there have been many innovations in making gamers groove to crazy beats over the decades. 


## Arcade games
DanceDanceRevolution has long been the centerpiece and a staple machine at any commercial arcade or game center. The machine's sleek paired with a spectacularly loud play interface will catch the attention of anyone within a twenty feet radius even in the busiest environments. Known as Dancing Stage in Europe, the Benami classic started a new genre of gaming, with series such as Pump it Up and In the Groove taking off in its trail.

For gamers really into sick beats but not that much into excercise, Bemani, the company behind DDR, has game series such as Beatmania (IIDX), Sound Voltex and Pop 'n Music all with unique input devices to enable feeling the rhythm in your soul in many different ways.

## Playing at home
In the late 90s to early 2000s, many rhythm game nerds weren't satisfied with only playing at arcades, sprouting new and exciting ways to enjoy rhythmic smacking of keyboard keys in the comfort of your home through arcade game clone software such as the StepMania engine or Be-Music Source players. Twenty some years later, legend says these strange, beat-crazed communities still thrive in their own small corner of the internet.



Games designed specifically for home-use such as Guitar Hero and osu! have also seen commercial success as fun and easy ways to get your groove on. As testament to the 2000s rhythm game craze, old guitar controllers and soft DDR mats can be found plagueing flea markets globally even today.


Most modern rhythm games aimed at consumer use are built to be played on smart devices such as phones or tablets or on a PC setup. ## Rhythm gaming in Finland
Bemani infamously hasn't sold their rhythm game machines to Europe, but that hasn't stopped the europeans from yearning for them. For a mostly complete list of arcade machines around the world, see [Zenius -I- vanisher's map](https://zenius-i-vanisher.com/v5.2/arcades.php).


For places to play rhythm games in Finland, Tarry recommends the following:

[Sugoi](https://www.sugoi.fi/)

A commercial japanese-style game center in Malmi, Helsinki. Open on weekends, Sugoi is the place to try out arcade rhythm games the way they're meant to be played. All machines are in free-play and accessible after a modest day ticket.

[McKylä](https://mckyla.tanssipelit.fi/)

A private-owned garage maintained by hardcore veterans of the Finnish dance game scene. Featuring three top-of-the-line modded ITGmania setups and a Pump it Up cabinet, McKylä is the heavenly haven of Finnish dance gaming. To play at McKylä, refer to the schedule on their website for open door days or get in contact with a tenant.

[Tarry arcade](https://tarry.fi/arcade)

Opening January 2026, Tarry's own arcade will operate in Nekala, Tampere. The association's arcade will operate similarly to McKylä, with the addition of more rhythm games besides dance games.
